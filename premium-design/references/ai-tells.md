# AI tells: what makes a design read as generated, ranked

## Summary

- The heaviest tells are structural: a stock category section sequence [14], a default font as the brand voice [13], purple/violet as the brand colour [11], motion with no job [11], and shipping one-shot generator output [9]. Fix these first.
- Next tier: eyebrow pills over headings [7], icon-on-top feature card grids [7], effect stacking [7], emoji as icons [6], no real product on the page [6], glass as the main visual [5], decoration that carries no information [5], adjective-only prompts [5].
- Swapping one default for the next (purple to cream + serif + terracotta, Inter to Space Grotesk) fixes nothing [4][2]. Derive the look from the subject's own world, then pass the squint and swap tests.
- Target 0-1 default patterns per page. In a scan of 1,590 Show HN sites, 46% had 0-1, 32% had 2-3 and 22% had 4+ [5].

Tags: `[N]` = independent creators stating it; `[1★]` = a single source, kept because it is canonical or fills a gap; `[1]` = a single community source, reported but not canonical. Source ids (a04, a06...) and full backers: [evidence.md](evidence.md). Detector thresholds cited as (a06) come from Krebs' Show HN scorer; (a04) from Bakaus' Impeccable.

Never decided by this file: the accent hue, the colour strategy, light vs dark, the display face and weight, the button shape, the layout families and the one signature move. Each comes from the brief and the subject's world. The rules below remove defaults; they do not supply a house style.

## 1. Layout and structure (top weight [14])

- **[14] Stock section sequence.** The SaaS version: announcement bar, pill badge, gradient hero with product mock, logo cloud, stat strip, three identical feature cards, "3 simple steps", testimonial cards, three-tier pricing with a highlighted middle, CTA strip, 4-column footer, floating chat button. Also a closing FAQ of 3+ `<details>` in the bottom half [1★ a06]. *Spot:* the component list reads `Hero, LogoCloud, Stats, Features, Steps, Testimonials, Pricing, CTA, Footer`, or every section reuses one pattern. *Fix:* derive the information architecture from this page's content; each section must answer a question the visitor actually has; use at least 4 distinct layout families on a landing page. Every category has its own stock sequence. DTC product page (synthesis): split hero with price, CTA and a secondary link; a craft or origin story; a feature or parts list; care; a centred closing CTA over a 2x2 "in the box / delivery / returns / guarantee" grid. Break at least two of its parts: service terms go under the price they qualify, a guarantee beside the claim it backs. Component-level repetition counts too: the same short-title-plus-muted-paragraph list in 3+ sections is one pattern repeated (slop_check `repeated-item-pattern`).
- **[14] One gap value everywhere.** *Spot:* the same `gap-8`/`space-y-8` on every stack; an item equidistant between two groups. *Fix:* gaps by relationship: 4-16px inside a group (labels 4-8px from inputs), at least 2x the inner gap between groups (24-80px), 80-96px between desktop marketing sections, about 32px between mobile app sections. Every value from one 4/8-based token scale [11].
- **[7] Patchwork sections.** One section suddenly adopts its own font, button style, colour world or photo treatment (a rainbow motion-blur band under a white hero). *Fix:* one design language across every section: same fonts, icons, button style, image grade.
- **[7] Misaligned chrome.** *Spot:* the sidebar header border and the main header underline sit at different heights; tab and sidebar labels off a shared axis. *Fix:* align every element to at least one other on shared horizontal and vertical axes; check by drawing guides along edges.
- **[4] Template hero skeleton in a new skin.** Three shapes: centred pill eyebrow + two-tone headline + sub-headline + pill CTA + ghost "Watch demo" + stat row + icon grid; copy-left/image-right split with a badge row (or, on a product page, eyebrow + sentence H1 + subhead + button and text link + fine print beside a product floating on the page ground (synthesis)); centred headline over a row of equal cards. *Fix:* change the structure, not the styling; see the hero context rule below. [1★ a29] An "Ask anything" chat input in place of a headline is the AI-startup variant: state the promise instead.
- **[3] Big-number hero or vanity metric strip.** *Spot:* 3-6 sibling cards each led by a number of 22px or more and 10 characters or fewer (10K+, 99.9%, 4.9, 3x) (a06). *Fix:* show real content; put a measured number, with context, next to the claim it supports.
- **[2] Everything exposed at the top level.** Every row carries reveal, copy, status, revoke and delete; columns duplicate another tab. Variant [1★]: the same KPI row repeated on the dashboard, sidebar and detail pages. *Fix:* keep only the actions and columns the task needs; move the rest into row menus, detail views or the relevant tab; show each KPI once where it matters.

## 2. Type (top weight [13])

- **[13] Default font as the brand voice.** Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Lato, Times New Roman, or the system/browser/design-tool default. One source puts default type behind most of the "looks AI" feeling. *Spot:* `getComputedStyle(h1).fontFamily` resolves to one of these, or Tailwind's `font-sans` was never configured. *Fix:* headings in a face with character, chosen from at least 3 candidates auditioned in the real layout [10]; body and UI may use that family's Text cut or a neutral workhorse. Native Apple apps are the exception (context rules), but an HTML mock of one shows its `-apple-system` titles in Inter or Segoe UI on every non-Apple screen.
- **(synthesis) This skill's own fixture faces.** The clean fixtures in `scripts/fixtures/` each load a face; agents following the skill reuse them, so they become its house style. Leave them out of the audition (slop_check `fixture-face`).
- **[7] Eyebrow or pill badge above headings.** "Introducing X", "Powered by next-gen AI", a launch date. *Spot:* `text-[11px] uppercase tracking-[0.18em]`, mono at 10.5px with `tracking-[0.22em]`, or a small `rounded-full border` pill over the H1. *Fix:* fold its words into the heading or body; keep at most one per three sections, and only when it carries information the headline lacks.
- **[4] Tracked all-caps as the default label style.** *Spot:* `text-transform: uppercase` with letter-spacing of 0.5px or more (a06); the typical AI value is 0.18-0.22em. *Fix:* sentence or title case at normal tracking for labels, links, headings; caps only on one-line tags, tracked +0.03 to +0.1em [11].
- **[3] Oversized italic display serif hero.** Fraunces, Instrument Serif, Recoleta, Playfair, Newsreader italic. *Fix:* sans display by default for tech and product work; serif only under the conditions in the context rules.
- **[3] One accented word in the headline.** Serif italic, second colour, gradient, highlighter background or underline on one word. *Spot:* a headline of 32px or more and 140 characters or fewer with 2+ styled runs (a06). *Fix:* one face, one style per headline; emphasis from size or weight; put spot colour on the focal object or CTA instead.
- **[3] Monospace as a "technical" costume** on display text, body, subheads or small labels. *Fix:* mono only for literal code, CLI output, IDs, hashes, timestamps, tabular data and specs; for aligned numbers use tabular figures in the main face.
- **[2] The "safe-distinctive" LLM favourites.** Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Geist, Syne, Bricolage Grotesque, Sora, Young Serif, Plus Jakarta Sans, DM Sans/Serif, Outfit, Playfair; a04 adds Cormorant, Lora, Crimson, Newsreader, Space Mono, IBM Plex, Instrument Sans. *Spot:* the heading font is on the list, or listed faces cover 25% or more of page text (a06; one accent word of about 6% is tolerated). *Fix:* use one only when no other face meets the need, and write that reason into DESIGN.md. "Books, so serif" or "tech, so mono" is not a reason. Contested: a05 recommends Geist and Outfit as Inter replacements; never as the display voice. The replacements a01 and a05 recommend (Satoshi, Clash Display/Grotesk, Cabinet Grotesk, Geist Display) are the next rotation and need the same written reason.
- **[2] Gradient-filled text.** *Spot:* any `background-clip: text` gradient (`bg-gradient-to-r ... bg-clip-text text-transparent`), a centred H1 of 40px or more in gradient, or 4+ elements with visible gradients (stop alpha above 0.05) (a06). *Fix:* solid colour; emphasis from weight or size.
- **[1] Reflexive 700+ display bold** (a10, community brand files: Apple 600, Linear 600 display / 400 body, Vercel at most 600, Stripe 300). The tell is a reflexive 700+ bold on a face not drawn for it, typically a wide geometric sans. *Fix:* don't bold a display face to 700+ by reflex; use the weight its display cut was drawn for. A heavy condensed or normal-width poster face [3], a single wordmark-as-graphic and native emphasized title styles (Bold Large Title) are exempt; display weight is a per-brief choice (typography.md Context rules 6-7).

## 3. Colour (top weight [11])

- **[11] Purple/violet as the brand colour.** Purple-to-blue or purple-to-magenta gradients on hero backgrounds, headline text, preview frames or buttons (worst on white); indigo/violet fills on CTAs, active pills, badges. *Spot:* hue 250-300 degrees, saturation above 0.25, lightness 0.15-0.85 on a filled CTA at opacity 0.5 or more; one instance triggers it (a06). In Tailwind: `indigo-*`, `violet-*`, `purple-*`, `from-purple-* to-blue-*`. *Fix:* take the palette from the brand's own world; neutral active states (a black active pill with grey count labels).
- **[5] Mixed-temperature neutrals.** Warm greys next to cool ones; a blue CTA in a warm-grey site. *Fix:* one neutral temperature across the product; by default tint neutrals toward the accent hue by hue only (OKLCH chroma about 0.005-0.02) [6]. True-neutral greys are correct where content colour must be judged (colourways, photography, fashion, data viz).
- **[4] Many hues, no single accent.** The logo, selected tab, user name, status pills, badges and each stat card or icon chip get their own hue (one AI dashboard had about 15 coloured elements). *Spot:* count saturated hues outside semantic states; more than one is the tell. *Fix:* neutrals plus exactly one accent [9], used only on primary CTAs, key controls, selected states and the one item that needs attention [8]. Chart palettes are a separate data token set.
- **[3] A reflexive flat pure-white or cool grey-white canvas, or a flat stock dark theme.** *Fix:* choose a deliberate surface temperature, or let a texture or photo carry the ground. Pure #FFFFFF is fine beside a second surface tier (a tinted off-white, a brand band or an inverted section); on the web, ink is near-black (#1d1d1f to #111), never #000 on #FFF [6]. True-neutral greys are fine where content colour must be judged.
- **[2] Reflexive near-black hero (#0A0A0A to #111).** *Fix:* pick the dark value deliberately (Linear's #010102 is chosen, not defaulted) or use a brand-tinted ground, and only when the use scene argues for dark.
- **[1★] Permanent dark mode with medium-grey body text.** *Spot:* 12% or more of body text (12-20px, weight below 600) under 7:1 on a background of luminance below 0.2 (a06). *Fix:* body at 4.5:1 minimum and 7:1 on dark surfaces [8]; muted text never below 4.5:1 (#6e6e73 on white is about 5:1).

## 4. Motion and interaction (top weight [11])

- **[11] Motion with no job.** Every button, icon and section animated; confetti, shimmer or wiggle on routine controls; purposeless carousels; decorative glass transitions. *Fix:* keep a motion only if you can state its job in one sentence (feedback, state change, hierarchy, depth or context, story pacing); everything else stays still.
- **[4] Linear timing; [1★ a04] bounce or elastic easing.** *Fix:* strong ease-out such as `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for UI; springs only for direct manipulation (drag, swipe, sheet release) with bounce about 0-0.15.
- **[3] Ambient decorative motion.** Cursor-following edge glows, buttons that chase the cursor, meteors, scroll-following SVG lines, pulsing status dots, fake blinking cursors, marquee or ticker bars, image hover zoom or rotate. *Spot:* `animate-pulse`/`animate-ping`/`infinite` on non-live elements, `mousemove` listeners driving glows, marquee components. *Fix:* delete; pulse only indicators tied to real live data.
- **[2] Every section fades or slides up on scroll** (often on timers), and every card has a hover transition. *Spot:* `whileInView` or `data-aos="fade-up"` on each section. *Fix:* content visible by default; spend un-triggered motion on one orchestrated moment; stagger only real lists.
- **[1★] Broken generated interactions** (YC review of vibe-coded sites). Hovers that fade, dim, vanish or jump vertically; arrows that move backwards; dropdowns that close across a gap; buttons that morph shape; a pointer cursor on non-buttons; auto-advancing carousels and panels; scroll-jacking. *Fix:* QA every generated component by hand: hover states pop or advance, all equivalent items are clickable with consistent chevrons, native scrolling is never overridden.

## 5. Process (top weight [9])

- **[9] Shipping one-shot generator output** (v0, Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, Figma Make, any single prompt). Models return the average of their training data. *Fix:* treat it as a draft; rework type, colour, layout, flows, states and details; budget the last 10-20% by hand [7]. In one case about 15 minutes of fixes (emoji, borders, purple, icon sizes, gaps) moved a page from generic to polished.
- **[7] Letting the model invent the UI from a blank prompt.** *Fix:* start from a proven base (a screenshot of the exact section, or its DESIGN.md) taken from a premium player in this brief's own niche: a fragrance house for fragrance, a respected local restaurant for a restaurant, a docs site this audience already uses. Use Apple, Linear, Stripe or Vercel (the awesome-design-md corpus) only when the brief is in their category, or every brief converges on their house style. Keep only structure and spacing ratios, then replace brand, names, copy, numbers, colours and imagery.
- **[6] One direction only.** *Fix:* generate at least 3 radically different directions (light vs dark, different layout, different star) before converging.
- **[5] Adjective prompts** ("beautiful", "modern, clean, trustworthy, premium"). An adjective names a wide area of design space; a concrete reference pins one spot in it. *Fix:* a DESIGN.md with primary-colour and typography tokens, references and real copy [7]; tell the model it drifts to on-distribution output and name each default with a concrete alternative [3]; steer one dimension per instruction, in the order type, colour, spacing, motion [4].
- **[5] No counter-steering budget.** *Fix:* before building, list the parts you would produce for any similar prompt and revise them; before shipping, run the checks at the end of this file until 0-1 default patterns remain. Never ship the default look with only the logo swapped.
- **[4] Slop-free but characterless.** Removing defects is the floor, not taste. *Fix:* make opinionated, brief-specific choices about palette, type and layout, and push one dominant move through every element [4].
- **[2] Replacing one default with the next trending one** (Space Grotesk everywhere, the current "anti-slop" look). *Fix:* vary typeface, light vs dark and overall aesthetic from project to project, on the axes the brief's target emotions [8] leave open; expect every fresh antidote to become the next tell, including this skill's own overcorrections (the looks table, last row).

## 6. Components (top weight [7])

- **[7] Feature grid of icon-on-top cards** (3-up, 3x2, a 4-6 tile uniform bento), each with icon tile, bold title, one-line promise. *Spot:* `grid-cols-3` of identical cards each opening with a tinted rounded icon tile. *Fix:* build the section from the content: 2-column zig-zag, asymmetric grid, scroll-pinned or horizontal-scroll section, masonry, real product graphics; icons beside headings, not in tiles above them. Moving the icon beside the title, or dropping it, leaves the same uniform grid when 4+ equal title-plus-blurb items sit in a regular grid (synthesis): show each item in context (the rule applied to a real line, the part on the drawing), vary sizes by importance, or give each a distinct real visual (slop_check `uniform-item-grid`).
- **[6] Emoji or Unicode glyphs as icons** in nav, sidebars, buttons, filter pills, card headings, copy. *Spot:* 40% or more of sidebar links contain emoji, or nav has 3+ (a06); regex `\p{Extended_Pictographic}` in UI strings. *Fix:* one drawn icon family at one stroke width (1.5 or 2.0) [3]; SF Symbols on Apple platforms. User-chosen page emoji (Notion) are content, not icons.
- **[4] Cards everywhere.** Hero, every feature, testimonials and pricing each in a rounded shadowed card; nested cards; stacked outlines; one radius and one `rgba(0,0,0,.1)` shadow on everything. *Fix:* separate with spacing, type and dividers; a container only when the content is an object, is interactive, or holds media; one radius scale by role (8px inputs, 12-16px cards, 20-28px large containers, full pills only for actions), nested concentrically (inner = outer minus padding).
- **[4] Library and builder defaults shipped as identity.** shadcn/ui create defaults (Style Nova, Base Neutral, Inter, Lucide, Radius Default), Tailwind UI marketing sections, Framer glow presets, Aceternity gradient borders (a 1px purple-to-transparent hairline [1★]), raw v0/Lovable output, browser-default range sliders styled only with `accent-color`, and hand-drawn icon sets that copy Lucide geometry stroke for stroke. *Fix:* keep the library as the engine; restyle every token, font, icon, radius and effect.
- **[3] Coloured side stripe** on cards, callouts, list items or selected states. *Spot:* `border-l-4 border-<hue>`; a saturated border of 2px or more that is over 1px wider than the other sides, or a 2-10px `::before` stripe covering 60% or more of an edge (a06); grey, black and white are exempt. *Fix:* a full fill change, a weight change or a neutral treatment.
- **[3] Placeholders left in.** Gradient-circle initial avatars, generic logo tiles, styled-text wordmarks in logo walls, the builder's stock logo. *Fix:* real headshots, the actual product photo, real SVG logos (Simple Icons, devicon), a real wordmark.
- **[2] Borders on every button, card, input, badge, sidebar group, heading and footer.** Variant [1★]: stark white 1px outlines on dark cards. *Fix:* group with whitespace first [5] (the Stripe dashboard uses two borders); draw remaining separators at low contrast; keep 3:1 only on boundaries that identify a control or its state.
- **[2] Uniform default effects** (dark mode, gradients, glows, glass, drop shadows, rounded corners on everything). *Fix:* state radius and effects explicitly in the brief (for example "Radius: None") and add an effect only where the reference calls for it.
- **[1★ gap-fill: tom is loading] shadcn inset or segmented tabs as top-level app navigation.** *Fix:* a sidebar or top nav for pages; segmented tabs only for sub-views (follows the conventions rule, [8]).
- **[1★] The four-point sparkle as the "AI is here" symbol** (a29). *Fix:* remove every instance; design one replacement mark for the AI feature.

## 7. Decoration and imagery (top weight [7])

- **[7] Effect stacking to show effort.** Shadows, gradients, glows, hover animations, video, floating shapes, WebGL/3D piled up. *Fix:* keep an effect only if it speeds comprehension or expresses the concept; heavy effects in one or two sections; test each AI-suggested effect with "would I spend a week hand-coding this?".
- **[6] Abstraction instead of the product.** Orbs, glows and abstract marketing art where the product should be. *Fix:* real UI screenshots, real outputs, real interior pages (Linear: full-width panels at 16px radius, never cropped). [2] Never fake the preview: no placeholder task lists, terminals or dashboards (lorem rows, meaningless bars), no typing animations of a fake prompt and answer, no "v0.6.2-rc.1" or "last sync 4s ago" inside mockups. A faithful HTML render of the product's real output and screens is the product, not a fake (synthesis of this and [6]); leaving it out for flat tinted boxes fails [6].
- **[5] Glassmorphism as the main visual or default card.** *Spot:* `backdrop-filter: blur()` of 2px or more on translucent cards 150-820px wide, sticky edge bars exempt (a06); `bg-white/5 backdrop-blur-xl border-white/10` cards over gradients. *Fix:* solid content surfaces; glass only on the floating functional layer (Apple sub-nav: 52px, #f5f5f7 at 80% opacity, `saturate(180%) blur(20px)`) with a solid fallback.
- **[5] Decoration that carries no information.** Diamonds or dots before titles, green "Live" pills, loud indicator badges, cards that do nothing, rotated text, "Scroll to explore", locale or weather strips, "v1.4.2"/"Build 0048" footers, "BRAND. MOTION. SPATIAL." strips. *Fix:* delete; a status dot only for real state, one per section at most; finish with a removal pass that takes away one more thing [8].
- **[5] Text-and-chrome-only page** (landing and marketing pages; docs and reading pages are text-led by design). A landing page of text, a dashboard mock and an FAQ, with no image of the audience's world. *Fix:* at least 2-3 real images (hero, product or in-use, supporting). Test: if the first viewport still works with the image removed, the image is too weak.
- **[3] Coloured glows, neon halos, zero-offset coloured shadows.** *Spot:* a box-shadow with blur of 15px or more, saturation above 0.3 and alpha 0.1 or more; two on a page trigger it (a06); any zero-offset chromatic halo on dark (a04). *Fix:* neutral shadows with a real offset and soft blur; on a coloured ground, a darker low-chroma shade of that ground.
- **[3] Mesh, aurora, spotlight or radial glow backgrounds,** above all the top-centre purple-to-blue/cyan glow behind the H1. *Spot:* saturated `radial-gradient` washes or `blur-3xl` blob divs behind the hero. [1★ a04] Hairline grid or stripe backgrounds are the same move. *Fix:* atmosphere from photography, lit renders, real texture or a flat tinted ground; grid overlays only on real canvas, map or blueprint surfaces.
- **[3] Stock photography, generic icon sets, generic generated graphics.** Unsplash placeholders, hands on laptops, smiling teams. *Fix:* own product shots, commissioned photography or brand-specific illustration, all graded with one treatment [6].
- **[2] Flat SVG or abstract vector illustration where a photograph belongs.** *Fix:* tell the generator "no illustrations" and use real photos of the product or subject.
- **[1★] Generic elevation.** One heavy blurred drop shadow on cards (Vercel instead stacks `0 1px 1px #00000005, 0 2px 2px #0000000a` plus an inset border); hard zero-blur offsets (`4px 4px 0`) outside a real neo-brutalist brief (a04). *Fix:* tonal surfaces for in-flow elevation; layered shadows only on things that float.

## 8. Copy (top weight [7])

- **[7] Interchangeable hero headline.** "Create Games with AI", "Close better deals faster". *Fix:* say what it is, who it is for and the outcome, specific enough that no competitor could reuse it, 2-3 lines or fewer on desktop, plus one CTA whose label names exactly what happens. [1★ a29] H1s built on "Meet your AI copilot/teammate/agent" and product names ending in -ly, -ify or "AI" are the same failure.
- **[4] Buzzwords and fake data.** streamline, empower, supercharge, world-class, next-generation; "Not X. Just Y."; John Doe, Acme, Nexus, SmartFlow; lorem ipsum; invented or suspiciously round metrics (99.99%, 10x). [3] Also Elevate, Seamless, Unleash, Revolutionize, Game-changer, Delve, Tapestry, cute performative labels ("Field notes", "Quietly in use at") and leaked prompt or design commentary. *Fix:* name what the product literally does; numbers only from real data or labelled as mock; replace all placeholder copy before judging a design [4]; if deleting 30% of the copy improves the page, keep deleting.
- **[4] Self-numbered sections.** "00 / INDEX", "001 · Capabilities", "SECTION 01", "01 / 4", "Step 1 / Phase 01". *Spot:* 3+ consecutive numbers starting at 1 within 140px (a06). *Fix:* name the topic in plain language; verb-nouns for real steps (Install, Configure, Ship).
- **[4] Generic action labels** (Submit, Continue, OK, Yes/No). *Fix:* the specific action in active voice ("Save changes", "Save API key").
- **[3] Em-dashes in UI copy.** *Spot:* 8 or more, around 1 per 500 characters (a04); target zero (a05). *Fix:* period, comma, colon or parentheses; ranges with a hyphen (2018-2026); no "WORD (em-dash) fragment" labels.
- **[2] Meta strings joined by middle dots or pipes** ("A · B · C"). *Fix:* one separator per line at most, or restructure.
- **[1★] A trailing arrow on every link and button** ("Start building free" plus an arrow) (Anthropic). *Fix:* add a chevron only to the secondary text link that needs direction.
- **[1★] Subheads that restate the page title** ("Manage your API keys and monitor their usage"). *Fix:* delete; the user knows why they are there.
- **[3] The brief pasted into the page** (the "prompt language" of copy_content.0.19): the audience or positioning line of the brief reused verbatim, and DESIGN.md left as a comment in shipped markup. *Spot:* slop_check `--brief` (`brief-echo`) and `design-commentary`. *Fix:* the audience in its own words; the spec in its own file.
- **(synthesis) Uniform declarative cadence.** Every heading a full-stop sentence of similar length, slogan pairs ("Made once. Kept forever.") with no fact only this brand can claim, three paragraphs in a row opening with the brand name, and "No X, just Y" in any form. *Spot:* slop_check `heading-cadence`, `template-copy`. *Fix:* statements for the hero and at most 2 heads; plain labels or questions for informational modules; promote the most specific line on the page to the H1.

## The AI looks that must never be a default

Each look is legitimate only when the brief pins it or the subject's world really is that [2]. Never ship the same look twice in a row.

| This skill's overcorrection (synthesis, from a blind test) | A cool grey-blue or green canvas on a warm brief (an osteria, a perfume), Bootstrap-blue rectangular CTAs, a functional face (cockpit, signage) as display, a sentence H1 near 4.5rem, section heads at 2x body, paperwork props (stamps, batch cards, Gantt hours), a rotated reading sheet | Clean scan, tidy spacing, no presence: it reads as a booking app or a docs page | Temperature from the emotions; the product or name at display scale; section heads 2.5x+ body; the promise shown in a scene; props from the desirable layer of the world |

## Tests: squint, swap and friends

- **Squint test [4].** Put the page beside products from unrelated categories (a social app, accounting software, a marketing site, a weather app). If you cannot tell them apart, if the aesthetic is guessable from the category alone, or if it matches a look in the table above, rework until the domain shows.
- **Blur test [2], radii [1★ NN/g].** Blur the screenshot at 5, 10 and 20px. The first thing the eye lands on must be the most important element, not the heaviest or most colourful block.
- **Swap test [4].** If the first viewport or layout could belong to a competitor, redesign it. If the page works with only your logo swapped in, it is the default look [5].
- **Similar-prompt test [5].** Work through a similar prompt; if the plan lands in the same place, that part is a default. Run it on the signature move too: if a category leader's main screen already shows it, it is the category's vernacular, not a signature.
- **Reference-world test (synthesis).** From the first-viewport screenshot alone, without DESIGN.md, name the reference world. If you can only name the category, the concept lives in the comments, not on the page.
- **Effort test [7].** Would you spend a week hand-coding this effect? If not, cut it.
- **Five-year test** (from the trends conflict resolution). Would the choice still look deliberate in 5 years, and would you choose it if no competitor were doing it?
- **Memory test [2].** What would a visitor describe an hour later? If the answer is "a gradient", the first viewport shows no product.

## Context rules (where sources conflict)

- **Brand typeface.** Default: headings in a licensed face that is unusual in the category but classic in structure (with optical sizes), or a loadable stand-in of the same class until it is licensed (typography.md section 1); body in its Text cut or a neutral workhorse. Use SF through system text styles in native iOS/macOS apps, and one neutral sans with tabular figures in dense data tools. SF cannot be self-hosted on a non-Apple website, and `system-ui` renders SF only on Apple devices.
- **Serif display.** Default: sans display for tech and product. Use a serif when the brand is editorial, literary, hospitality, fashion/luxury or heritage: upright, outside the overused pool, on cream only with a written reason and never with a terracotta accent, used throughout rather than as an accent word; Didones only at about 32px and up.
- **Mono.** Default: none outside literal code and data. Never for eyebrows or section labels, even on developer products.
- **Eyebrows.** Default: none. Keep one when it carries information the headline lacks (product name above a tagline, article category, news date), at most one per three sections, readable size, caps tracked only +0.03 to +0.1em.
- **Purple vs cream.** Swapping one for the other fixes nothing. Default: derive the palette from the subject's world. Cream is valid only when the subject is made of it (paper, bakery, ceramics, hospitality). Always replace the terracotta/rust accent; default to a sans display. A serif display on cream needs a written reason and must be upright and outside the overused pool.
- **Restrained vs committed colour.** Default: tinted neutrals + one accent for action, selection and focus + semantic states. Use committed colour (one hue owning a section) at section or campaign scale when colour is content: a product colourway, a campaign, a colour-identity brand. The accent stays the only interactive colour.
- **Neutral tint.** Default: a barely tinted ramp (OKLCH chroma about 0.005-0.02, hue from the accent). Use true-neutral greys when content colour must be judged accurately (product colourways, photography, fashion, colour tools, data viz).
- **White and black.** Default: near-black ink on white or off-white. Use pure #000 only for renders lit on black, OLED media viewers and `prefers-contrast: more`.
- **Light vs dark.** Default: light. Go dark when the use scene argues for it (pro or cinematic renders, media tools, a developer tool's own UI, night use, space or automotive), then with brand-tinted off-black, lighter surfaces for elevation, accent chroma 0.15 or less, no neon accent unless it is the brand's, no purple glow and no glass stack, and different from the Linear-clone row on at least two of colour, face and imagery. Docs and dev-tool marketing default to light with an OS-following dark theme. One inverted section per page is the safe use. Product apps support both appearances through tokens.
- **Gradients and texture.** Default: none. Use a gradient only when something physical motivates it (light across a surface, a vignette seating a product, a ramp of the brand hue), at hero or section scale and low contrast; never on buttons or text, never as a top-centre glow. Grain only at a few percent opacity on photo or flat fields.
- **Glass.** Default: solid surfaces. Use glass on sticky nav, toolbars, tab bars, sheets and overlays with a solid fallback; physical refraction only on native Apple via system components.
- **Static vs animated.** Default: motion only with a one-sentence job; product state changes at 150-300ms. A marketing page gets one signature story motion, a short entrance (600ms or less, never delaying content or LCP) and real hover and press states; that meets "not static" without ornament. A looping hero video only when the footage is the content: muted, with a poster frame and a pause control.
- **Easing.** Default: `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`, 150-300ms, exits faster than entrances, no overshoot. Use springs for gestures and momentum, bounce about 0-0.15.
- **Whitespace vs trend minimalism.** Default: generous whitespace on marketing and editorial pages, but only with strong hierarchy. If small grey type floats in emptiness, raise the type scale and contrast instead of adding elements. Tools and dashboards take compact density from the lower scale steps (4/8/12/16).
- **Removal vs character.** Remove ornament, keep character: one dominant move carried by elements that already work (typeface, committed colour, imagery, scale, one signature motion). A brand motif appears in 3-4 places at most, each doing a job.
- **Hero structure.** Let the star decide. Strong product or place imagery: a centred product-led hero or a full-bleed image with verified-contrast overlay. Software or services: a left-aligned stack with a real product visual. A split is fine without the badge row, stat row and ghost "Watch demo". Type crossing the product is the signature for one hero per site, not a default.
- **Cards and bento.** Default: spacing and type separate content. A bento is acceptable only when every tile holds a distinct real visual or measured fact and tile sizes vary by importance.
- **Social proof.** Default: no section exists because the category has one. Proof must be verifiable and sit next to the claim it supports; logo walls suit B2B; highlight a pricing tier only if it truly is the recommendation.
- **Conventions vs risk.** Interaction, navigation, forms and controls stay conventional. Distinctiveness belongs in visual voice, imagery, type, composition and copy.
- **References and templates.** Mine them for structure and spacing ratios only, never for visual identity.
- **Spot colour vs accent word.** Spot colour goes on the focal object or action. A coloured headline word is allowed only in poster or campaign art where the word is the subject, once per site, in a solid colour.
- **CTA colour.** One primary per view. The brand accent if the brand has one; near-black when imagery supplies the colour. A black pill on an otherwise grey minimal page reads as template. Secondary is a text link; a filled + outline pair only for two real parallel actions.
- **Icons.** Use them where they clarify repeated scannable data (spec rows, metadata, status, tab bars) at 16-20px, not on headings or feature tiles. One library, one stroke, restyled; avoid unmodified Lucide-in-shadcn as the brand's icon language.
- **Radius.** Choose sharp or soft from the reference world (the product's hardware, packaging, signage, the brand mark), not from the category label. Sharp tends to read luxury or editorial, soft reads friendly. Never both in one view.
- **Shadows.** Tonal surfaces for in-flow elevation; layered neutral shadows only on what floats; product cut-outs get a soft contact shadow only.
- **A clear brief wins [2].** A pinned aesthetic, era, font or palette is followed exactly, even when it matches a look above. These anti-patterns govern only the axes the brief leaves open.
